 THE SIXTEEN TASK FAMILIES
================================================================================
 How the energy literature divides the field. Each has its own objective, method
 and metric. Detail and Brazilian source for each: report/tarefas-dados.tex

   F1  Resource assessment ............. available irradiance and wind
   F2  Generation and load forecasting . solar, wind, load
   F3  Asset inventory and detection ... locate and date plants from imagery
   F4  Operations monitoring ........... generation and emissions without telemetry
   F5  Reservoir and hydropower ........ area, level, volume from satellite
   F6  Performance and degradation ..... performance ratio, soiling
   F7  Siting and land-use conflict .... siting, agrivoltaics
   F8  Energy access and demand ........ electrification, load by region
   F9  Adequacy and reliability ........ non-served risk, ENS
   F10 Failure and unavailability ...... generator unit failure rate
   F11 Price and marginal cost ......... CMO and PLD as estimands
   F12 Interchange, flow and constraint  subsystem flows, network curtailment
   F13 Quality, frequency, ancillaries . frequency deviation, reserve
   F14 Marginal emissions .............. marginal factor per MWh, MCTI/SIRENE
   F15 Methane and fugitive emissions .. point plume from satellite
   F16 Right-of-way, vegetation, line risk corridor encroachment, ignition

[x] F11 · Half-hourly CMO forecast one day ahead
    First experiment in family 11, which until 06/09/2026 had the data on disk
    and no use as an estimand. data/raw/cmo/ was listed as orphan raw in this
    list; it stopped being so.

    Target: CMO in R$/MWh for the 48 half-hours of day D, four subsystems,
    forecast issued at 12:00 on D−1. Test Jun/2025–Aug/2026: 85,248 rows,
    444 days, 15 monthly recalibrations, 41 features. Adversary: seasonal naive
    from the price literature — D−1 Tue–Fri, D−7 Sat–Mon — MAE 49.09.

    MEASURED GATE, NOT ASSUMED. Half-hourly CMO is published BEFORE the day it
    refers to: the file downloaded at 10:08 on 27/08 already carried that day's
    48 half-hours. Load and interchange lag 1.2 d; EAR and ENA, 2.2 and 2.3 d.
    Hence the gate — CMO through D−1, load through D−2, hydrology through D−3 —
    and it applies EQUALLY to model and naive. Each lag is ONE observation,
    file modification date against last record: ONS does not stamp publication.

    TIE IN MAE, GAIN IN RMSE.
        direct ......... MAE 51.32  skill  −4.54%  CI [−12.39; +2.74]
        residual ....... MAE 50.04  skill  −1.94%  CI [ −6.50; +2.26]
        two-part ....... MAE 48.71  skill  +0.76%  CI [ −6.72; +7.70]
        level oracle ... MAE 40.42  skill +17.66%  CI [+15.28; +20.21]
    In RMSE on the same test: two-part +16.2%, direct +15.3%.
    All three models have CI crossing zero in MAE. None is distinguishable
    from repeating the previous day.

    THE MECHANISM, MEASURED. CMO sits on the floor in 14.0% of test half-hours.
    The naive is a realised path and hits the floor exactly in 71.6% of them,
    median error 0.00. A single regressor returns the conditional median and
    hits 19%, paying 13–17 R$/MWh there. Modelling the floor explicitly recovers
    66.0% and moves skill from −4.54% to +0.76%.
    Absolute-error quantiles: naive wins at q25 (2.25 vs 5.13) and q50
    (12.10 vs 19.78) and loses at q90 (157.62 vs 131.75) and q95. The model
    errs less than it on only 40.7% of rows and still has 16% lower RMSE.
    CONSEQUENCE: on this target the choice of loss chooses the winner. MAE was
    declared before looking at results, and is the price-literature metric.

    THE POSITIVE CONTROL COST A CORRECTION. The oracle shifted by the MEAN of
    the day's residual gives +0.9% with CI crossing zero — the design would
    look unable to detect gain, and the tie above would mean nothing. The loss
    is MAE and the constant that minimises it is the median. Switched, +17.66%
    with CI entirely above zero. Same rule E-despacho-termico had already
    recorded, applied to an oracle instead of a baseline.

    DISPERSION. Monthly skill from −68.2% (Dec/2025) to +38.7% (Jan/2026),
    above zero in 10 of 15 months; two bad months carry the aggregate. By
    subsystem: S +3.6%, SE +1.5%, NE +0.9%, N −2.7%. That dispersion produces
    the 14-point-wide CI, and is what blocks a stronger claim.

    AT TWO DAYS THE MODEL WINS. The horizon control — same design, target at
    D+1, whole gate shifted with it — brought more than the control:
        D ..... model 48.71  naive 49.09  skill  +0.76%  CI [ −6.72; +7.70]
        D+1 ... model 62.68  naive 74.89  skill +16.31%  CI [+10.64; +21.87]
    Both errors grow, as the control requires. But the naive loses 25.80 R$/MWh
    from one day to the next against 13.96 for the model: the most recent
    realised path is what it has, and is worth little at two days. The naive
    is unbeatable only at ONE day.

    ABLATION DOES NOT IDENTIFY, AND THE REASON WAS MEASURED.
        A0 calendar + own CMO .............. 20 feats  −4.87%
        A1 + other subsystems' CMO ......... 28 feats  −1.01%
        A2 + load and interchange .......... 36 feats  −9.50%
        A3 + hydrology (full) .............. 41 feats  +0.76%
    With colsample_bytree = 0.8 LightGBM samples columns by POSITION, so
    permuting the same columns is changing the seed. The first run concatenated
    families in the order they were summed and gave, for the SAME sets:
    −3.66%, −1.84%, −2.69% and +4.40%. Difference 0.83 to 6.81 points, same
    order as the ladder steps (3.86, −8.49, 10.26).
    Fixed to canonical order, A3 reproduces step 3 exactly (+0.76%), which
    closes the explanation. CONSEQUENCE: with one seed, no claim about which
    source carries skill holds, and the experiment makes none. It all fits
    inside the 14-point CI of step 3.

    Feature-permutation null: −206.91%, worse than climatology.

    Controls C1–C6, all blocking, all passed. C3 erases from sources everything
    after the issuance instant and rebuilds features with the same function as
    step 1: 40 sampled days, 307,200 cells, zero divergences.

    C1 FAILED ONCE, and what it found was not measured: each hole in the source
    costs THREE panel days — itself, the next day that loses the naive's D−1,
    and day seven later that loses D−7. That is 8 holes and 24 days off the
    panel. The threshold was 8. It stays fixed at 24 and not computed: if the
    source worsens and the number changes, the control fails instead of absorbing.
